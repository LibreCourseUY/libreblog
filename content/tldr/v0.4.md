# v0.4

Back on schedule. Here's what actually mattered in tech this week (July 13–15, 2026).

### Tech News

#### Linux

**Linus Torvalds** shipped **Linux 7.2-rc3** (July 12) — mostly driver patches, networking tweaks, and filesystem fixes, with stable tracking for mid-to-late August. Less fun: two decade-old privilege-escalation flaws got disclosed — **GhostLock** (`CVE-2026-43499`, a use-after-free lurking since 2011) and **Januscape**. Basically: *patch your boxes.*

#### AI / Dev Tools

**OpenAI launched GPT-5.6** (July 13) as a three-part lineup — Sol, Terra, and Luna — notably shipping *after* a Commerce Department review, the first big model out with explicit government clearance. Meanwhile **Claude Code got computer use**: it now drives a sandboxed browser and opens apps straight from the terminal, and Sonnet 5 became the default across all plans. The theme: *agents are moving from "autocomplete" to "actually operates your machine."*

#### Platform Wars

**SpaceX's $60B acquisition of Anysphere (Cursor)** is heading toward a Q3 close, and it's showing up in usage — Cursor's share slid from ~41% to ~26% while Anthropic's climbed toward 50%. **GitHub Copilot** also moved all plans to usage-based AI Credits. The fight is now about **who owns the coding-agent surface**, not just who has the best model.

#### Infra

The capital side is wild: **SK Hynix raised a record $26.5B** for AI infra, **Meta** puts its custom "Iris" chip into manufacturing in September (targeting 14 GW by 2027), and **Tower Semiconductor** committed $3B to advanced fabs in Japan. The takeaway: *chips, memory, and power are the new bottleneck, and everyone is buying ahead.*
